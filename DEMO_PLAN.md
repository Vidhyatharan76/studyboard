# 🎬 Studyboard: 6-Minute Demo Video Script & Walkthrough

> **Note for Presenter**: Talk naturally like you're explaining your project to a friend or mentor. Don't sound like a sales pitch. Speak at a relaxed, conversational pace.

---

### ⏱️ Quick Schedule & Setup
- **Windows**: Have two browser windows side-by-side at the start (Window A on the left, Window B in Incognito on the right).
- **Pre-drawn items**: Have a quadratic equation (`y = x^2 - 4x + 3`), a drawn molecule, and an imported PDF page already placed off to the sides so you can smoothly pan to them later.
- **Center**: Keep a clean space in the middle to write live during Minute 2.

---

## 🟢 MINUTE 1 (0:00 – 1:00) | Two Windows, One Live Board
*Criteria: Collaboration & Real Problem*

### What to show on screen:
- Start with two windows side-by-side.
- In Window A, type room name: `Math & Chem Study Group` and click **Create Room**.
- Click **Invite** in the top header, copy the code.
- In Window B, paste the code and click **Join**.
- Point out the **"2 Online"** pill lighting up in both headers.
- Draw a quick stroke or drop a sticky note in Window A—watch it show up instantly in Window B.

### What to say (Natural Voice):
> "Hey everyone, welcome to Studyboard. We wanted to kick off this video with two browser windows side-by-side because collaboration is literally the entire point of what we built.
>
> Here’s what normally happens when my friends and I try to study together online: someone shares their screen on Discord or Zoom, but only one person can actually touch the screen. The rest of us just sit there watching. If you use a regular online whiteboard, it's basically just flat digital paper. It can't read what you write, it can't solve equations, and if someone gets stuck, the whole group stops while everyone opens five different tabs for Google, Desmos, and textbooks.
>
> We built Studyboard so a study group can jump into the exact same room in about two seconds with a simple invite code, and work together on an infinite canvas that actually understands what we're studying."

---

## 🟢 MINUTE 2 (1:00 – 2:15) | Writing and Using the AI Tutor Live
*Criteria: Core Functionality & AI*

### What to show on screen:
- Maximize Window A so the screen is clear and sharp.
- Pick up the **Pen tool**. Write an equation or problem on the blank area (e.g. `3x + 9 = 24`, or a quick physics formula).
- Switch to the **Lasso tool**. Draw a box around your handwriting.
- The action menu pops right up above the selection. Click **AI Tutor**.
- The side drawer slides in. Let judges see the loading animation and the clean results:
  1. The transcribed equation.
  2. The numbered steps.
  3. The core concept summary.
- Click **"Pin Explanation Note to Canvas"**.
- The drawer closes, and the sticky note pops onto the canvas right next to your handwriting.

### What to say (Natural Voice):
> "Now let's look at the coolest feature: our built-in AI Tutor. 
>
> Let's say I'm working through a homework problem here on the board. My handwriting isn't perfect, but that's fine. If our group gets stuck or we just want to double-check our work, I don't have to leave the tab. 
>
> I just grab this Lasso tool, circle the handwriting, and hit 'AI Tutor'.
>
> What happens right here is that Studyboard crops that section of the canvas and sends it to Gemini 2.5 Flash on our server. Within two seconds, it reads the handwriting, checks the math, and gives us three things: the exact problem it read, a step-by-step breakdown of how to solve it, and the actual concept behind it so you're not just staring at a final answer.
>
> When we're happy with it, we just click 'Pin Explanation', and it drops a readable note right onto our board. And what’s great is this isn't only for math—it works for physics formulas, chemistry questions, biology notes, or history prompts too."

---

## 🟢 MINUTE 3 (2:15 – 3:30) | Live Math Graphing & 3D Molecules
*Criteria: STEM Tools & Creativity*

### What to show on screen:
- Pan smoothly over to your pre-drawn quadratic equation (`y = x^2 - 4x + 3`).
- Lasso-select it and click **Plot Math Graph**.
- The graph modal pops up: point out the vertex, roots, factored form, and the coordinate plot.
- Close the graph modal. Pan over to the sketched molecule (e.g., Ethanol or a benzene ring).
- Lasso-select it and click **View 3D Molecule**.
- Grab the 3D model with your mouse and rotate it in circles to show the 3D depth.

### What to say (Natural Voice):
> "We also realized that for STEM subjects, flat drawings just don't cut it. Students need to see how things actually behave in 2D and 3D.
>
> So we added two interactive tools right into the canvas:
>
> First, math graphing. Over here, we have a quadratic equation. If I lasso this and click 'Plot Math Graph', Studyboard calculates the vertex, finds the real roots, factors it, and draws out the full parabola on an interactive coordinate plane. You don't have to copy-paste anything into a separate graphing calculator.
>
> Second, chemistry. When you're learning organic chemistry, looking at flat 2D stick drawings on paper makes it really hard to understand how atoms actually fit together in space. Over here, we drew a simple molecular structure. I can lasso it, click 'View 3D Molecule', and boom—it renders an interactive 3D ball-and-stick model. We can rotate it, look at the bond angles from any side, and discuss it together as a group."

---

## 🟢 MINUTE 4 (3:30 – 4:30) | Classroom Workflow & Clean Design
*Criteria: Usability & Practical Design*

### What to show on screen:
- Pan over to an imported lecture slide or PDF worksheet.
- Grab the **Highlighter tool** and highlight a sentence or diagram on the slide.
- Draw an arrow pointing from the slide to a sticky note.
- Demonstrate canvas controls: zoom in and out with the bottom slider (`40%` to `150%`), hold Spacebar to pan smoothly.
- Double-click a note or text to edit it; hit Delete to remove something.

### What to say (Natural Voice):
> "In terms of design, we tried to keep everything as clean and frictionless as possible. 
>
> In real school life, students are usually working off actual handouts, lecture slides, or past exams. So we made it so you can drop PDFs or image slides right onto the canvas. Here, we imported an actual problem sheet. We can highlight directly over it, sketch arrows, and drop sticky notes next to tricky questions.
>
> We also chose a dark theme on purpose to keep eye strain low during late-night study cram sessions. The toolbar floats along the bottom so it never blocks your workspace, and we wrote custom color logic so that whether you're adding text or different colored sticky notes, the font color automatically adjusts to stay high-contrast and easy to read. 
>
> You can pan around with the spacebar, zoom in and out smoothly, and double-click to edit any text directly."

---

## 🟢 MINUTE 5 (4:30 – 5:15) | Who This Helps & School-Life Impact
*Criteria: Real Impact*

### What to show on screen:
- Zoom out so the judges see the full board: the imported assignment on the left, student handwriting in the center, the pinned AI steps, the graph, and the 3D molecule.
- It looks like a real, rich, productive study session.

### What to say (Natural Voice):
> "So where does this actually make a difference in school?
>
> First, for everyday study groups. Instead of people texting messy phone photos of paper homework in a Discord chat, everyone is actively working on the exact same board in real time.
>
> Second, for TA hours and tutoring. A tutor or teaching assistant can hop into a room with a student, look at where they're getting stuck, and use the AI tutor to generate alternative explanations if a student doesn't understand the textbook's wording.
>
> And third, for exam prep. Everything on this board stays saved, so when test week rolls around, your study group has a complete visual record of every solved problem and concept."

---

## 🟢 MINUTE 6 (5:15 – 6:00) | How We Built It, AI Disclosure & Learnings
*Criteria: Learning & Tech Stack*

### What to show on screen:
- Switch to your GitHub repo tab or show the clean app header and project overview.
- Then flip back to the active canvas for a final closing look.

### What to say (Natural Voice):
> "To wrap up, a quick look at how we built this and what we learned along the way:
>
> Studyboard is built with Next.js 15 App Router, React, and TypeScript, styled with Tailwind CSS. The drawing canvas runs directly on the HTML5 Canvas API, and the 3D molecular viewer uses WebGL.
>
> For our AI disclosure: we run Google Gemini 2.5 Flash server-side using the `@google/genai` SDK. That keeps our API keys completely safe on the backend. When a user lassos a section, we crop that canvas area, send the image to Gemini, and ask it to return strict JSON with the parsed text, steps, and core concept. We also used Google AI Studio tools during development to help us debug our canvas coordinate transforms.
>
> What did we learn? The hardest technical challenge was definitely the canvas math—making sure that when you zoom in to 150% and pan around, mouse clicks and lasso crops still map accurately to the underlying canvas pixels. We also learned how to tune our vision prompts so Gemini gives back reliable, concise explanations rather than rambling.
>
> We had a ton of fun building Studyboard, and we really think it can make online studying a lot more engaging. Thanks so much for watching, and we'd love to hear your questions!"

## 💡 Quick Tips Before You Record:
1. **Don't rush**: You have a full 6 minutes—speak comfortably with natural pauses.
2. **Smile while talking**: It naturally makes your voice sound friendly and confident.
3. **If you make a small stumble**: Don't panic or restart the whole video—just pause for 1 second, repeat the sentence naturally, and keep moving!
